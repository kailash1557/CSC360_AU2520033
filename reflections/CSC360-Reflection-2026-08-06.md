# CSC360 Reflection — 06/08/2026

This session followed the first introductory class and focused on the basics of digital images and how graphics actually work. I learned that an image is made up of pixels, the smallest unit of light in a display, each emitting a certain color to form the full picture together. We also discussed how color itself works, since an object appears a certain color because it reflects that color of light while absorbing the rest. Videos are just a sequence of images shown at a certain rate, and eighteen to twenty four frames per second is generally the point where our eyes stop distinguishing individual frames and perceive smooth motion.

We also covered different types of images beyond regular photographs, including solar, sonar, x ray, and thermal images, along with how each is captured and used in fields like biomedical research, military applications, and navigation systems. This helped me understand that as computer scientists we need to think of images more broadly than just camera photos.

Vector images came up as well. Unlike regular images, they are stored as mathematical descriptions of shapes rather than fixed pixels, so they do not lose quality when scaled to different sizes.

We learned some basic graphic transformations, including translation for moving a shape without changing its size, scaling for resizing it, and rotation for turning it around a point.

Outside of the core imaging topics, we also touched on SSH and CLI basics along with an intro to keygen, and skimmed the table of contents of our reference textbook, Klawonn's Introduction to Computer Graphics Using Java 2D and 3D. We discussed real world applications of computer graphics, like visualizing gene expression data from experiments, which reinforced the idea that an image is really just another way of storing information. We also got a practical note on how to draw a perpendicular line using drawLine(x1, y1, x2, y2) given two points and their intersection.
